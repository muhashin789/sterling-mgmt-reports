The $2,500 benchmark

One sewing line running one day must earn $2,500 of CM value. Everything below is measured against that single number.

Yearly surplus or shortfall against the benchmark

Shortfall = (2,500 − earned per line-day) × that buyer's line-days. It is what the account cost in capacity terms over the period, not a cash loss.

What $2,500 a day actually requires

The earning identity behind every number in this report:

Earned $ per line-day = CM $ per SMV minute × efficiency × operators × working hours × 60

With a typical line of 75 operators working 10 hours, one line-day holds 45,000 available minutes. So:

Read it as a menu, not a rule. At 65% efficiency the price has to be $0.0855 per minute to clear $2,500. At 75% efficiency the same line clears it at $0.0741. Every route to the benchmark is a trade between those two columns.

Improvement plan — buyer by buyer

For every buyer below the benchmark, three routes to $2,500 a day, each calculated from that buyer's own numbers. The three are alternatives, not a sequence — hitting any one of them clears the bar.

How to read the three routes

Route A — price
Raise CM per piece
Keep the same SMV and the same efficiency, and ask the buyer for a higher CM. This is the merchandising conversation.
Route B — method
Cut the SMV
Keep the same CM per piece and take work out of the garment. The rate per minute rises without touching the price. This is the IE job.
Route C — floor
Lift efficiency
Keep price and SMV, run the line better. Only realistic where the required number is under about 78%.

How this report and the ranking were built

Written in plain English so anyone can check the work, repeat it next month, or argue with it.

Step by step

The six scoring standards

Each buyer is measured against a fixed factory standard. Score on that line = actual ÷ standard × 100, capped at 100. The six scores are then multiplied by their weights and added up to give a total out of 100.

Why cap at 100: this is a scorecard against a standard, not a competition. A buyer who pays double the benchmark rate is excellent, but it does not cancel out a floor running at 55% efficiency. Capping keeps one very strong number from hiding four weak ones.

Data quality and caveats

Earned $ is CM value booked — pieces × CM rate. It is not FOB and it is not margin, so this report can say which buyer earns most per unit of sewing capacity, but not which one is most profitable. Efficiency and DHU are simple means of the daily values, which reproduces the factory's own yearly summary exactly. SMV is weighted by pieces produced. CM rates were updated from the actual costing sheets supplied wherever a style matched; every earned figure is rebuilt as pieces × CM. October 2026 is 24 days only.
